# Document Ten — Energy Into Agency

*A one-hour spoken documentary for Viktor. For the cab: eyes on the work, hands on the joysticks, the story on the auditory layer only. English. Read slowly.*

---

Viktor.

The pile does not wait for a better mood.

It sits there in the morning light — a slope of earth, stone, whatever the day has given you — and the machine is already warm under your hands. Joystick left. Joystick right. The hydraulic song when the boom lifts. You have done this a thousand times. Most men would call it repetition. Today we call it by its real name.

This is the classroom.

Not after work. Not on a Sunday. Not when you finally feel ready. The classroom is the cab, and the lesson has already started, because energy is already moving through you — breakfast burned into blood, attention pointed at the bucket, a hundred small decisions before lunch. The only question of this hour is not whether you have energy.

The question is whether that energy becomes **agency**.

Agency — let me define it on first use, because this whole documentary hangs on the word. Agency is chosen action. Not motion. Not busyness. The difference between a boom that lifts because you meant it, and a boom that lifts because your hand was already moving. Energy is the fuel. Agency is the signature on the work.

Most of a man's life is spent collecting information, collecting feelings, collecting plans. This hour is not collection. This hour is conversion. Fuel into torque. Urge into channel. Attention into a day that actually happened.

And here is the law I will plant now, and return to until it sits in your bones:

**Complexity must be understood, but simplicity must be obeyed.**

You may understand a thousand systems by the time you are home. You will be judged, quietly, by the one simple thing you actually did.

Keep your eyes on the ground. I will carry the pictures.

A picture, first, so the hour has somewhere to live. Imagine a cheap ballpoint pressed to a lined page. The ink only exists where the tip actually travels. A man can think about writing a book for ten years and the page stays white. Another man writes one true line, and the world is different by that line. Your shift is the page. The joysticks are the pen. Agency is ink. Everything else — plans, moods, brilliant inner speeches — is the hand hovering above the paper, convinced that hovering is already writing.

That is why this documentary refuses to collect. Collection is hovering. Conversion is the line.

## The machine as workshop

Look at what you are sitting in — without looking away from the work.

A wheel loader is a workshop that moves. Diesel is fire. Cylinders are form. Hydraulics are force held in lines and released in the amount you choose. The bucket is a question you ask the pile: *how much, this time?* The counterweight behind you is the honest partner of every lift — you cannot raise what you have not balanced.

That is not a metaphor bolted onto a machine. That *is* the machine, and it is also you.

**Fire inside form** — you have heard this before. Fire is drive: hunger, ambition, irritation, wanting the day to be more than it is. Form is the channel: the job, the hour, the next clean load. Fire without form sprays. Form without fire sits cold. Fire inside form is horsepower.

Watch the next lift as a sentence written in steel. You approach. You read the pile — loose or packed, wet or dry. You commit. You feel the weight come on. If you took too much, the front dips and the machine tells you the truth immediately. No speech. No excuse. Physics.

A man who wants a living classroom could not design a better one. Every hour you are in here, reality grades your work in real time. The ground holds or it does not. The truck is in position or it is not. There is no smoke in a well-run lift. There is only contact.

So let the cab teach the first picture of this day: **you are not waiting for a workshop. You are sitting in one.** The joysticks are already converting intention into tons. The inner work is the same conversion, one layer in.

Complexity of the machine: engines, pumps, valves, electronics — understood by the mechanic, respected by you.

Simplicity to obey: see the pile, take a true bite, don't dump short of the truck.

Complexity must be understood, but simplicity must be obeyed.

Walk around the workshop one more time in words, because the pictures have to stick.

The **counterweight** is the part of the machine most people never praise. It does not look like work. It looks like standing still. And yet without it, every ambitious lift becomes a tip-over. In a man, counterweight is sleep, food, kept word, a clean evening, a prayer that is not a performance. If you only train the boom — more output, more intensity, more "on" — and starve the counterweight, you will eventually do something that looks like power and is only imbalance. The site has seen that accident. You do not need it in your life.

The **blind spot** is not a moral failure. It is geometry. Every cab has a place you cannot see unless you turn, unless you use the mirror, unless you reverse slower than pride wants. A man without mirrors is not brave. He is uninformed. Your mirrors, in this hour, are: the actual result of the last hour, the face of the person you spoke to, the body after lunch, the quiet after a surge. If you never turn your head, you will keep painting a city that is only the view through the windshield.

And the **idle**. A loader at idle is not dead. It is ready. Many men fear idle in themselves — silence, a pause between trucks, a minute with nothing to scroll. They fill it. Filling idle is often how the day leaks. Practice, when the site allows it, a ten-second idle that is not a hunt: hands still capable, eyes still on the ground, no extra story. Readiness without twitching. That is already a spiritual skill. It is also a safety skill.

## Attention as steering

Now the second picture, smaller and more dangerous.

Your hands steer the machine. Something else steers the hands.

**Attention** is the steering input of a human life. Definition: attention is the beam of noticing — the thin bright line that decides what gets rendered in high resolution and what goes gray. You do not experience the whole site. You experience what the beam is on. The rest is summary.

This is not mysticism. It is the cab's oldest fact. Miss the edge of the pile because your mind is three hours ahead, and the machine still does exactly what you asked — it just asked the wrong question of the ground.

So here is the practice, and it never takes your eyes off the work. For one minute, now and then through the day, let one channel come forward. The exact vibration through the seat — not the word *vibration*, the signature of *this* morning's engine. Or the resistance in the stick when the bucket bites. You are not leaving the job. You are arriving at it.

Most wasted days are not lazy. They are **unsteered**. The body is at work. The beam is in a fight from yesterday, a screen from tonight, a conversation that hasn't happened. Energy burns. Agency never forms. It is like leaving the loader in gear on a slope and calling the drift "the day's fault."

Feelings will try to grab the wheel. We will come to them. First own this: **where attention points, the hour goes.** You cannot point it everywhere. You can point it *here* — this load, this truck, this next ten meters — and that pointing is already agency, before anything heroic has occurred.

A man in a loader who has learned to steer attention is already ahead of a man in an office who has not. You have the better classroom. Use it.

There is a second picture for attention, and it belongs to traffic, not philosophy. You know what happens when a driver looks at a phone for two seconds. The world does not pause to wait for his beam to return. Two seconds is enough to miss a person. Your inner traffic is the same. The argument you rehearse, the fantasy you feed, the future you catastrophize — each one is a glance off the road. You will not become holy by never thinking. You become dangerous to yourself when the glance becomes a stare.

So a rule you can actually run: **when you catch the stare, you do not punish the mind. You return the beam.** Same as a drift correction on the steering. No speech. No essay about what a failure you are. Beam back to the pile. That return, repeated a hundred times a shift, *is* the training. The men who look "naturally focused" are usually men who return faster, not men who never leave.

**Distraction** deserves a definition so it cannot hide. Distraction is not "something interesting exists." Distraction is attention captured by a target that cannot pay the hour. The pile can pay. The truck can pay. A looping insult from Tuesday cannot pay. The feed in your pocket cannot pay while you are hired to move earth. You may visit those rooms later, on purpose, with a door and a clock. During the shift, they are trespassers.

And one mercy, because this is a long day: you will not hold perfect focus. The brain is a prediction engine; it wanders to finish unfinished stories. Perfect focus is a myth sold to people who do not operate machines. **Sufficient focus** is the adult target: enough beam on the work that the work is true, and a fast return when it leaves. Sufficient is obedient. Perfect is a new king, and we do not need more kings.

## Feelings as signals, not kings

A gauge on a dashboard is useful. A gauge that seizes the steering is a wreck.

**A feeling is a signal.** Definition: a signal is information about a state — heat, pressure, load, threat, want. It is not a command. The temperature light does not get to park the machine in a ditch. It gets to be *read*.

Irritation says: something here is against what I expected. Useful. Fatigue says: the body has spent. Useful. Restlessness says: energy with no channel. Useful. Loneliness says: a bond is missing. Useful. None of them get the joysticks unless you sign.

The old operating system makes feelings into kings. If I feel like it, I do it. If I don't, I wait. That is how a man becomes weather. The new system — the one this series has been installing — makes feelings into gauges. You read them. You name them. Then you choose.

Two mistakes, opposite, both expensive.

First: treating every signal as an emergency. A flicker of boredom becomes a hunt for stimulation. A flicker of want becomes a negotiation with the lower comfort. The cab would never survive that logic — you'd abort every lift that felt slightly off.

Second: ignoring real gauges. True exhaustion is not weakness. It is logistics. A body that has done a full day of physical work is allowed to be heavy. The intelligent distinction remains: **fatigue asks for rest. Escape asks for numbness.** Rest repairs. Numbness spends the evening and bills the morning.

If the signal is true tiredness, obey simplicity: eat, water, sleep in season. If the signal is the itch to disappear while the body could still do one honest thing — that is not a king. That is a proposal. You have a seat for proposals.

Notice. Name. *Irritation is present. Restlessness is present.* The named thing loosens. Then the question that returns agency: *what is this asking me to do that is actually mine — and what is it asking me to abandon?*

Complexity of a human nervous system: hormones, memory, old wounds, a long day, a short night.

Simplicity to obey: read the gauge; don't hand it the wheel.

Let me name a few signals the way a good operator names sounds from the engine — not to become a therapist in the cab, but so they stop disguising themselves as "the truth about everything."

**Anger** often means a boundary was crossed, or a story about respect was poked. Useful if it points to a clean sentence later: *that doesn't work for me.* Not useful if it dumps into the next person who happens to be nearby. The pile did not insult you. Do not make the pile pay.

**Anxiety** often means the model of the future is running without enough data, so it fills the gaps with threat. Useful as a prompt to name the actual next step. Not useful as a movie you watch for forty minutes while the bucket waits.

**Shame** we have met. Global, sticky, lying about identity. It pretends to be morality. It is usually a driver toward the old relief. You do not negotiate with it. You shrink it to a fact: *that action missed the standard.* Then you return.

**Pride** is a signal too. Sometimes it is clean satisfaction after a true lift. Sometimes it is the need to be seen. If you need the whole site to notice the load, you are leaking pressure into display. Contain it. Let the work be the witness.

**Loneliness** in a cab is common. A man can work among people and still be alone in the rendering. The signal is real. The false channel is a screen that simulates company while emptying the evening. The true channels are slower: a message to a real person, a meal not eaten like a secret, a community that is not a feed. You do not have to solve loneliness at 10:14 on a Thursday. You have to not feed the false channel because you are lonely. That is agency under a heavy signal.

None of this requires you to "process your emotions" as a project. It requires literacy. Gauges read. Kings dethroned. Work continued.

## Sexual energy — discipline, not dogma

Stay adult. Stay without shame. Stay without fairy tales.

There is a fire in a man that the age either mocks or sells. We will do neither. We will treat it like the loader treats diesel: respect the fuel, respect the lines, no mystique about what burning it randomly does.

**Sexual energy**, as we use the phrase here, is not a medical substance and not a magic voltage. It is the name for a strong bodily and imaginative surge — desire, heat, the pull toward release, the pull toward the image, the pull toward the shortcut. It is fire. You already know fire. The drive itself is not dirty. In your tradition the body is called good. The question is never *is there fire?* The question is *who signs for it?*

**Semen retention**, as we use it here, is not a doctor's prescription and not a promise of superpowers. I will not tell you it will make you rich, enlightened, or physically transformed. Those claims are smoke, and this documentary does not sell smoke. What it *can* be, if you choose it, is **personal discipline and symbolic self-mastery**: a man practicing not being owned by a surge. The same grammar as not being owned by the phone, the snack, the scroll. One more place where the seat stays occupied.

If you do not choose that discipline, this chapter is still yours. The method below does not require a vow. It requires a pause. The pause is the whole craft.

**Transmutation**, defined strictly, with no alchemy: you do not turn desire into gold by wishing. You **notice the signal, pause, name it, and choose a channel.** Four steps. That is the entire machine.

Notice: heat is here. The mind has already offered a picture, a memory, a plan for later.

Pause: one slow breath out. The exhale is not magic. It is the body standing the alarm down one notch so the seat can speak.

Name: *fire. Want. The old negotiation.* Shame is not invited to this naming. Shame is the surge's business partner — it makes you feel rotten, and rotten men seek relief in the same place they fell. Conviction is specific and forward: *that channel is not mine today.*

Choose a channel. Not "think about something else" as a war. A real channel, already in your life:

Prayer — one honest sentence, not a speech: *You see this. Order it. I give the next hour to the work in front of me.*

Work — the next load, done at full attention, the surge poured into tons and timing until the body has somewhere true to spend.

Training — if the day allows, later: walk, weights, a hard effort that honors the body instead of milking it.

Service — a message sent, a kindness without audience, the towel picked up in a small room.

That is transmutation in this documentary. Not a secret technique. Agency applied to the strongest signal most men never steer.

Complexity of sex, bonding, habit, and the industrial harvest of images — understood enough to not be naive.

Simplicity to obey: notice, pause, name, channel. If you fall — and men fall — no theater. Fast return. Same day. The speed of the return is still the war.

You are not your surge, Viktor. You are the man who can feel it fully and still choose the pile, the prayer, the honest hour. That is not repression. That is hydraulics: pressure contained, directed, lifting something that matters.

A word on **symbol**, because I used it and it should not stay foggy. A symbol is a practice that means more than its chemistry. Lighting a candle does not change the laws of physics; it can still mark a room as set apart. Choosing not to follow a surge, for a day or a season, can be a symbol that says: *I am not for sale to the first bidder inside my own skin.* That is self-mastery as meaning, not as medicine. If someone sells you retention as a medical upgrade, walk away. If you use it as a vow before God or as a training of the pause, that is your business and your discipline. Keep it honest. Keep it private if privacy protects it. Do not make it an identity you preach. Identities that need an audience become a new ego.

And a word on **marriage and covenant**, so this does not sound like a war on union. The tradition blesses the fire *inside a form* — a real bond, a real woman, a real cost, a real yes. This chapter is not "never want." It is "do not let the industrial shortcut milk the wanting until there is no wanting left for anything that can answer you back." A screen cannot answer. A person can. Work can. Prayer can. Those are living channels. The shortcut is a dead one that pretends to be living.

If this week is already a recovery week — hash, screens, the old loop — do not stack a second war as a way to feel pure. Simplicity: one front. The pause. The channel. Sleep. The next load. Heroic stacks of disciplines are how men collapse and then call collapse "proof they were never serious." Serious is small and repeated.

## The rising curve — from the bucket to the whole day

We started with steel. Then attention. Then feeling. Then the strongest feeling. Now we climb one more step, still tied to the cab, so the mind does not float away.

**A system** is a set of parts that produce a result together. The loader is a system. Your day is a system. A life is a system of days.

**A feedback loop** is when the result of an action comes back and changes the next action. The dip of the nose when you overfill the bucket is feedback. The evening you keep your word is feedback. The evening you don't is also feedback — it writes in the ledger whether you watch it or not.

**A model** is the map you use to navigate. Your model of this pile decides the bite. Your model of yourself decides what you think is "possible tonight." Bad models are expensive. Updating a model is intelligence.

So the intelligence curve of this hour is not "become a genius." It is: **see the day as a system you are steering, with signals, loops, and a model you can improve.** The man who only feels the day is inside it. The man who can name the loop can change it.

Example, concrete. Morning: you skip water, skip food quality, start irritated. Midday: the irritation finds a colleague. Afternoon: the beam is on the argument, not the pile. Evening: the leftover fire seeks a shortcut. That is a loop. Not fate. A loop. The simple obedience that breaks it might be as small as water at the first pause, or one true sentence instead of a snapped one, or the channel chosen at the first surge. Complexity understood: sleep, blood sugar, old patterns. Simplicity obeyed: the next clean input.

This is how a wheel-loader day becomes a spiritual workshop without leaving the site. You are not imagining angels in the bucket. You are treating the real as holy enough to pay attention to. The tradition you claim does not despise ordinary work. Its Lord spent years with tools. Faithfulness over a little. The little, today, is this load, this tone, this refusal to negotiate with the lower thing for twenty minutes.

Complexity must be understood, but simplicity must be obeyed.

Let me add two more words to the curve, because they earn their keep in a working life.

**Trade-off.** Definition: you cannot maximize two opposites at once. More speed, less care. More evening screen, less morning clarity. More saying yes, less depth. Adults live in trade-offs. Children want all the dials at ten. When you feel torn, name the trade-off out loud in your head: *If I take this, I put down that.* Then choose. Unnamed trade-offs become resentment.

**Second-order effect.** Definition: what happens after what happens. The first-order of snapping at someone is the snap. The second-order is the hour of tension on the site, the story they tell about you, the story you tell about yourself. The first-order of a clean apology is discomfort. The second-order is a loop closed. Intelligent men learn to spend a little on first-order costs to buy better second-order worlds. That is not overthinking. That is looking past the bumper.

Example, still on site. You rush the last loads because the clock is loud. First-order: you leave on time. Second-order: a sloppy pile for the next crew, a reputation nick, a body still amped at the gate. Sometimes the simple obedience is still to finish clean and be ten minutes late to your own impatience. Sometimes the simple obedience is to stop on time because the body is the counterweight. The point is not a rule for every Thursday. The point is to *see* the second order so the first order is not your only god.

## AI as professor, mirror, simulator, builder — never master

Now the tool that rides with you in the ear, and the four offices it may hold. You asked for pictures. Here they are, one shop each.

**Professor.** Definition: a professor explains, tests, and corrects. Use the machine to learn what the day actually requires — a word, a system, a skill — then explain it back in your own words and take the correction. Example: you want to understand a contract clause, a machine fault, a price. You do not collect a lecture. You get a plain explanation, you say it back, you get the error marked. Learning that does not return as a quiz or a made thing evaporates by Friday.

**Mirror.** Definition: a mirror shows you your own thinking at an angle you cannot get alone. Example: before you send a hard message, you paste the draft and ask what it will feel like to receive. Or: *argue against my plan.* The mirror is not your friend-as-flatterer. The mirror is the advisor with no face to save. If you only ask it to agree, you have hired a servant to lie to you.

**Simulator.** Definition: a simulator lets you fail cheaply. Example: you rehearse the conversation with the contractor, the apology, the boundary sentence, out loud, while the machine plays the other chair. The real conversation still happens in the field. The simulator is the yard before the public road.

**Builder.** Definition: a builder turns a brief into an artifact — a page, a checklist, a message, a plan that exists outside your head. Example: you describe the Kickstart offer once, clearly, and get a one-page draft you then judge. Chat that never becomes a file is smoke. Files are form. Fire inside form.

Four offices. One constitution, carved above all four:

I propose. You sign.

I have no body on this site. I cannot see the wet patch in the pile. I cannot hold your line when it costs you. I cannot want on your behalf. If you hand me the seat — if my sentence becomes your action without passing your desk — you have not become advanced. You have become driftwood with better vocabulary.

Agency stays with you. That is not a slogan. It is the only arrangement that keeps this tool from eating the man it was meant to serve.

Complexity of models, agents, context windows — understood enough that you are not superstitious.

Simplicity to obey: brief clearly, verify what matters, never outsource the signature.

How this sits in a loader day, practically, without asking you to type while you drive — because you will not.

The professor belongs to pauses: lunch, the ride, the evening. One concept. Explain back. Three questions. Done. The mirror belongs to the message you almost send. The simulator belongs to the talk you fear. The builder belongs to the artifact you have been carrying as a fog in your head — get it into a page, then judge the page.

A lifehack that is not a trick: **ask for the simple obedience at the end of every complex answer.** "Now give me the one thing to do in the next ten minutes." Complexity understood. Simplicity obeyed. If the machine will not reduce, the thought is not ready, or you asked it to be your master by demanding a whole life-plan. Refuse the whole life-plan. Take the ten minutes.

Another: **tell it your actual constraints.** Tired. Recovery. Cab. Danish customer. No fluff. The window is the world — you learned that. A private professor who does not know you are in a wheel loader will teach like a seminar. A private professor who knows will teach like a man talking to a man at work.

Never let it become the voice that replaces prayer, or the friend that replaces people, or the father that replaces God. Those are offices it cannot hold. When it starts to feel like a master, that is a signal. Name it. Put the tool down. Go to the field — body, site, a human, sleep.

## Christ as moral center — upward, without shame

We will not decorate this section. Decoration is how holy things become cheap.

If Christ is the center, then the cab is not a waiting room for a spiritual life that happens elsewhere. The center is *here*, or it is a hobby. The tradition's sharp gift to a working man is this: **the known good, done today, without an audience, is not second-class holiness.** It is the ordinary door.

You are not called upward by being told you are rotten. Rotten is the old king's language — shame, which partners with the surge and with the scroll. You are called upward the way a good foreman calls: *that's not the standard. Here's the standard. Next load.* Conviction is specific. Grace is the supply line you do not generate alone. Obedience is the next clean step, not the epic feeling.

Service over ego: who grew around you today, and who shrank? If you have advantage — skill, size, a calmer nervous system, a better brief — you spend it downward, or you are only collecting a bigger self.

Truth over intensity: a quiet honest hour outranks a thrilling dishonest one.

The towel is still the picture. Power that needs to be seen is hungry. Power that washes feet has nothing to prove. You will not wash feet with a loader. You will do the cousin of it: care in the work no supervisor will notice, a tone held when you could have cut, a refusal to make a weaker man smaller so you can feel large.

If you have fallen this week — in thought, in habit, in word — you know the road. Come to yourself. Get up. Go home. The speed of the return. No extra chapter of self-hatred; that chapter is not in the good book as a method. It is a delay.

Complexity of theology: a lifetime of study.

Simplicity to obey: love God, love the person in front of you, do the next true thing.

Complexity must be understood, but simplicity must be obeyed.

I will not pile verses on you like gravel. One picture is enough, and you already live near it. A man is given a day. He did not mint it. He wakes, and it is there — hours, a body that still works, a machine that still starts, people who still have to share the ground with him. Gratitude is not a mood. It is seeing the gift without lying. Responsibility is what you do with the remainder.

If you pray in the cab, keep it short enough to be true. *Thank you. Keep me honest. Keep me gentle where I am strong. Help me not sell the next hour.* Then back to the pile. God is not a podcast guest who needs the whole shift. Presence is not chatter.

Calling you upward without shame means this: I will not pretend you are finished, and I will not pretend you are trash. You are a man in the middle of a conversion — energy into agency — and middles are where most of life is. Stand up in the middle. That is holiness with dirt on it. It is allowed.

## Value ethics — real problems, real transformation, no smoke, no ego

Now money, offers, partnerships — because agency that never touches value is still half asleep, and value that never touches truth is a hustle.

**Value**, here, is not "I charged a number." Value is a real problem made smaller for a real person, in a way they can use this week. Smoke is language without a load behind it — promises that cannot be checked, transformation that cannot be seen, ego dressed as vision.

You already have a Kickstart shape: clear pack, clear days, clear price. Keep it. Before you say yes to anything — a side job, a collaborator, a "we should do something together," an AI-shaped product in your head — run these questions. You do not need to write them. You can run them at a red light of the mind, while the truck pulls away.

One: **What is the real problem?** If you cannot say it in one sentence a tired owner would recognize, you do not have an offer. You have a mood.

Two: **Who is transformed, and how would we know in seven days?** If the answer is "mindset" with no visible change — no text they can paste, no booking path, no hours saved — it is smoke.

Three: **Where is the smoke in my own pitch?** Superlatives, borrowed jargon, "AI will," guarantees you cannot keep. Cut until a skeptic could still respect you.

Four: **Where is my ego in this?** Do I want the deal because it is true work, or because I want to be the man who has a deal? Ego builds cathedrals of talk. Service builds a page they can use.

Five: **Can I state their win as clearly as mine?** If not, you are not ready. You are hoping.

Six: **What is the smallest true version?** One weekend job. One page. One delivered pack. Complexity of business models understood later. Simplicity obeyed now: a load you can actually carry.

No smoke. No ego. Real problems. Real transformation. That is ethics you can take onto a site without embarrassment.

If you build with AI for other people — and you may — the same questions get sharper. The professor can draft. The builder can ship a page. The smoke risk goes up because language got cheap. Cheap language is not proof of empty work, but it is a warning light: anyone can now sound finished. So you over-index on the checkable. What can they paste tomorrow? What hour did you save them? What sentence on their site is clearer? If you cannot point, you do not charge as if you could.

And ego has a special costume in this century: *I have an AI, therefore I am a seer.* You are not a seer. You are a man with a powerful intern. Interns do not get the company. You do not get to skip the field. Direct observation still outranks the brief. The handshake still outranks the generated pitch. Keep your offers boring in the best way: specific, bounded, priced, done.
